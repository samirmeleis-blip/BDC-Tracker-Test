Methodology

The market in one paragraph

Non-traded BDCs let investors in freely but only let them out through quarterly tender offers. Each quarter a fund files an SC TO-I announcing it will repurchase up to a capped percentage of its shares — the floodgate, usually 5%. When the offer closes, the SC TO-I/A final amendment reports how many shares were tendered and what fraction was actually repurchased. When demand exceeds the floodgate, the offer is oversubscribed: tendering investors are rationed pro-rata, and the remainder are stuck until next quarter. Pantor Capital Solutions monetizes that stuck remainder — bridge loans to trapped investors, and capacity arrangements with the funds themselves.

Three grades of information

Every number on this site carries one of three provenance grades. The distinction is enforced visually everywhere — solid blue for facts, green for arithmetic, dashed violet for anything about the future:

  • Filed fact — transcribed from SC TO-I / SC TO-I/A filings on SEC EDGAR: date of tender, shares tendered, % of shares outstanding, floodgate, repurchase rate, CIK.
  • Derived — deterministic arithmetic on filed facts. Example: trapped shares = shares tendered × (1 − repurchase rate). No judgment involved; anyone with the filing reproduces the same number.
  • Model estimate — statements about the future or about desirability: projected tender windows, projected demand, oversubscription probability, opportunity scores, and every sentence of desk advice. These can be wrong.

The prediction model

The forward view is a deliberately simple, fully disclosed queue model — not a black box:

  • Next tender window. Funds run quarterly programs, so the projected window is the last final-amendment date + 91 days, ± 14 days.
  • Projected demand. Trapped demand doesn't evaporate — it re-queues. We assume 85% of refused shares re-tender and 60% of successfully executed demand recurs organically:

projected demand % = 0.85 × (tendered% × (1 − repurchase)) + 0.60 × min(tendered% × repurchase, floodgate)

  • Projected repurchase rate = min(100%, floodgate ÷ projected demand), assuming the floodgate holds.
  • Oversubscription probability is a logistic transform of projected demand ÷ floodgate, centered at 1.0×.

Every fund currently has exactly one completed cycle in the dataset, so all projections carry at most moderate confidence and are labeled directional. As the tracker accrues quarters, the same model re-fits on real cadence and re-tender behavior per fund.

The Pantor opportunity score

A 0–100 ranking of how much revenue potential a fund offers Pantor, built from three components:

score = 55 × min(1, trapped ÷ 0.85)  +  25 × min(1, (demand÷cap − 1) ÷ 6)  +  20 × min(1, log₁₀(trapped shares) ÷ 8.6)

  • Trapped-demand intensity (55 pts). The fraction of tendered shares refused exit. This is the direct measure of borrowers who need Pantor.
  • Cap pressure (25 pts). How far demand ran past the floodgate. Pressure predicts recurrence.
  • Scale (20 pts). Log of absolute trapped shares — a fund with 358M trapped shares supports a bigger book than one with 274K, at any intensity.

Tiers: Prime ≥ 70 · Strong ≥ 45 · Moderate ≥ 22 · Watchlist = fully executed but demand ≥ 85% of the cap (one cycle from locking up) · Low otherwise · Insufficient data when no cycle is on file.

Anomalies we flag

Some funds report demand above the floodgate yet repurchase 100% of tendered shares (e.g. a 6.8% tender against a 5% cap, fully honored). That means the board raised or waived the cap for the cycle. We flag these as cap breach anomalies: no trapped borrowers today, but the stated floodgate is soft and the demand pressure is real — classic watchlist material.

Reading the visuals

  • In the 3D market view, column height is √-scaled tendered demand (exact values in the tooltip); the violet plane floats at the 5% floodgate. In the per-fund gate simulation, the particle pass-through rate is the fund's filed repurchase rate.
  • In charts, red marks mean demand above the cap (a state, not a series); blue means cleared within the cap; violet + dashed always means model estimate.
  • Every chart has a table view (the toggle at its top-right) carrying the same numbers as text.

Data pipeline

The dataset is the Pantor Capital Solutions Tender Offer Tracker, refreshed weekly (Sundays, 6pm PT) from SEC EDGAR: for each fund's CIK, the most recent SC TO-I and its final SC TO-I/A are paired per cycle; a row updates only when a cycle's final amendment is confirmed (the "final amendment" checkbox on the Schedule TO cover). Funds with a new SC TO-I but no final amendment yet are intentionally left at their last completed cycle. Where a datapoint cannot be extracted from filings it appears as N/A, never imputed.
